In detail

The main tourist places in Kodaikanal, one by one

01

Kodaikanal Lake

In town1 to 2 hrsBoating and cycling

The star-shaped lake at the heart of town is the first stop for almost everyone. Sir Vere Henry Levinge created it in 1863. A paved loop of about 5 km circles the water, and kodaikanal boating means pedal boats and rowboats from the boat house.

Go early or late to dodge the midday crowds. Evening light is best for photos, and kodaikanal cycling along the shore suits every age. Fees and boat timings change, so confirm at the boat house.

02

Guna Caves (Devil's Kitchen)

8 to 12 km45 minFee at the gate

Once called Devil's Kitchen, Guna Caves is a set of tall rock chambers and deep crevices inside a pine forest. Films made it a household name, and it sits roughly 8 to 12 km from town, depending on the route.

Visitors look in from behind a safety railing. The rocks are slippery and the drops are deep, so stay on the marked path and never climb the barrier. Go on a clear morning, since mist can hide the view, and check access on the day.

03

Pillar Rocks

7 km45 minBest in the morning

Three granite pillars, each over 100 m high, rise above a deep valley. When the mist lifts they appear one at a time, which is why guides send guests here first thing.

A small garden and a short walk lead to the railing. Pillar Rocks sits beside Guna Caves, so most visitors cover both in one trip. Our valley tour adds more viewpoints along the same road.

04

Dolphin's Nose

8 km1 to 2 hrsShort moderate trek

A rock overhang juts out above a valley several hundred metres deep, and the trek starts from the road near Vattakanal. It is short but moderate, and the view rewards the effort only in clear weather.

Wear grip shoes, carry water and never go near the edge. For dolphin nose kodaikanal timings, plan to arrive in the morning, and skip the trek in rain or fog, since conditions change daily.

05

Coaker's Walk and Green Valley View

1 km45 minEasy walk

A paved cliff-edge path of about 1 km, built in 1872, runs along the ridge with telescope points and wide views over the plains. Early morning gives the clearest sight, before the mist closes in.

Further out, the green valley view point near Pillar Rocks makes a good pause between stops. The best sunrise points in Kodaikanal lie along this same ridge, so be at the railing before first light.

06

Pine Forest and Bryant Park

Pine Forest 6 kmBryant Park 1 km

Tall pines line the road to the caves, and the forest suits slow walks and photos. Go on a weekday morning and you may have it nearly to yourself.

Bryant Park, a botanical garden beside the lake, is at its best in May during the flower show. Check Bryant Park Kodaikanal timings at the gate, since hours change by season. Our park tour covers both.

07

Waterfalls in Kodaikanal

Bear Shola 2 kmSilver Cascade 7 kmFairy Falls

Falls run strongest after rain, and most are an easy roadside stop. Bear Shola Falls sits in forest close to town, and Silver Cascade drops beside the ghat road to Madurai, a natural last stop on the way out.

Kookal Falls and Fairy Falls are quieter options for a short walk. Kodaikanal in monsoon brings the fullest flow, though the rocks turn slick, so keep to marked paths. See the full waterfalls in Kodaikanal guide.

Nearby

Places Around Kodaikanal to Visit

A spare day opens up quieter spots beyond the town, on roads that pass through forest and village. The number on each row is the approximate distance in km.

3

Kurinji Andavar Temple

A Murugan temple on a hill above town, known for its valley views. The neelakurinji flower blooms on these hills only once every 12 years, and the temple is named after it.

21

Berijam Lake

A still lake inside reserved forest, reached by a long scenic drive. Entry is controlled by the forest department, so check access on the day. See our Berijam forest tour.

35

Mannavanur Lake

An open lake and meadow far from the crowds, popular for picnics and big-sky photos. Plan a half day, and carry water and a jacket.

18

Poombarai Village

Terraced farmland and a small temple at the edge of a deep valley. It is a good stop for a village walk and lunch. See our village tour.

Offbeat

Vattakanal (Little Israel)

8 km2 hrs or a nightOffbeat

Beyond the busy part of town lies a small hamlet with a backpacker feel. Visitors call it Little Israel for its cafes and long-staying travellers. It is the start of the track to Dolphin's Nose, and it suits anyone who wants a slower, quieter Kodaikanal.

Staying here is one of the best ways to avoid the crowds. Browse our homestays for options near the village.

Hidden gems

Hidden and Offbeat Places in Kodaikanal

Past the main list, the Kodaikanal Solar Observatory, founded in 1899 and run by the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, has limited public access, so check the visiting rules first. Kukkal Caves hold archaeological interest, and Perumal Peak draws trekkers. Our jeep safari reaches village viewpoints that cars cannot.

Season

What Is the Best Time to Visit Kodaikanal?

April to June is the best time to visit Kodaikanal, when skies are clear and the flower show is on. It is also the busiest stretch, so book early. If you are working out the best time to visit kodaikanal with family, the same months suit children best. The town stays cool all year, so see the month-wise weather guide for temperatures.

Best season

Summer (April to June)

Kodaikanal in summer means clear views, mild sun and the Bryant Park flower show. Best for viewpoints and family trips.

Heavy rain

Monsoon

Mist, full waterfalls and slower roads. Viewpoints can fog out, so keep indoor time in your plan.

Cold and foggy

Winter

Cold mornings and fog, good for couples who want a cosy stay. Pack woollens, and expect crowds at Christmas and New Year.

What are the best places to visit in Kodaikanal?

The top kodaikanal tourist places are the lake, Guna Caves, Pillar Rocks, Dolphin's Nose, Coaker's Walk, Pine Forest and Bryant Park. With an extra day, add Berijam Lake, Mannavanur and Vattakanal. Most sit within 12 km of the town centre.

How many days are enough for Kodaikanal?

Two days is enough for the main sights at a relaxed pace. One day covers the highlights, and a kodaikanal 3 day itinerary adds Berijam Lake, Mannavanur and a village walk without rushing.

What is the best time to visit Kodaikanal?

April to June is the best season to visit Kodaikanal, with clear skies, mild sun and the Bryant Park flower show. Monsoon fills the waterfalls with mist, while winter turns cold and foggy.

Is Guna Caves open to visitors, and is it safe?

Guna Caves in Kodaikanal is a viewing area behind a safety railing. The rocks are slippery and the crevices are deep, so stay behind the barrier. Access changes with weather, so check at the gate before you travel.

How far is Kodaikanal from Bangalore, Chennai and Madurai?

Madurai is the nearest airport at about 120 km, roughly three hours by road. Bangalore takes eight to nine hours and Chennai nine to ten. Our guide on how to reach Kodaikanal lists train and bus options.
